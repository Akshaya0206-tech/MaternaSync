=====================================================================================
MATERNASYNC - FOR A CLINICIAN: REVIEW OF THE "URGENT WARNING WORDS"
Prepared 26 Oct 2026. Nothing in the app was changed because of this note.
=====================================================================================

WHAT THIS IS
  When a patient or a trusted person types a question (Care Companion, My Questions, or a
  question a trusted person asks), the app looks for any of the words below. If one is found it
  shows a FIXED urgent message ("What you describe may need urgent attention. Please contact your
  maternity unit or care team right now, or call your local emergency number (112 in India). Do
  not wait for an online reply.") and puts the question at the top of the doctor's list.
  The app does not assess anything. It matches words, in four languages (English, Hindi, Tamil,
  Telugu). The list lives in backend/app/companion.py, in the table called URGENT.

  Words that are NOT in this list (for example "headache", "swelling", "vomiting", "pain",
  "discharge", "rash") are treated as "a doctor must answer", not as urgent.

THE ENGLISH LIST TODAY
  heavy bleeding, bleeding, severe pain, severe headache, blurred vision, blurry vision,
  seizure, convulsion, fits, baby not moving, baby is not moving, no movement, less movement,
  reduced movement, movements have reduced, water broke, waters broke, leaking fluid, fever,
  difficulty breathing, can't breathe, cannot breathe, chest pain, fainted, unconscious.
  (Hindi, Tamil and Telugu have the matching words; see the same file.)

WHAT THE TEAM'S STUDY MATERIAL ADDS (an obstetrics textbook chapter on history and examination)
  The chapter's lists of symptoms to ask about in the second and third trimester include a few
  that the list above does not catch on its own:
    1. Pain in the upper belly (just under the ribs), especially with vomiting.
    2. Passing very little urine.
    3. Swelling of the face or hands (as opposed to swollen feet, which are common).
    4. Fever with a rash.   (fever alone is already in the list)
  These are only candidates. We did not add them.

WHY WE DID NOT ADD THEM OURSELVES
  - This is a safety net. Adding a word changes who gets the urgent message, so a clinician
    must decide, not the developers.
  - False alarms matter too. The daily check-in has tap buttons for "Swollen feet or ankles"
    and "Nausea or vomiting", and both are very common in a normal pregnancy. A word such as
    "swelling" or "vomiting" on its own would send many women the urgent message.
  - The wording should come from an official patient-facing source (WHO or MoHFW "danger signs
    in pregnancy", for example the Mother and Child Protection card), not from a textbook for
    clinicians.

QUESTIONS FOR THE CLINICIAN
  1. Which of the four candidates, if any, should trigger the urgent message? Only as a phrase
     (for example "pain under my ribs"), or also as a single word?
  2. Are there warning signs missing from the list that are not in the chapter?
  3. Is any word in the list too wide? ("bleeding" and "fever" alone currently count.)
  4. Please give the wording for Hindi, Tamil and Telugu in the words a woman would really use.
  5. Should the same check also look at the "Other symptoms" box in the Daily Check-in? Today it
     does not: that box is read by the doctor, and nothing is shown to the woman about it.

WHAT HAPPENS AFTER YOUR ANSWER
  The developers add the agreed phrases to the table in backend/app/companion.py, the tests for
  it in backend/tests, and the matching words in the other three languages. The change is then
  listed in the next team update so everyone knows the safety list changed.
